How it works

Train. Track. Act.

The NHS buys Interval, clinicians prescribe it at diagnosis, and patients use it at home on a phone, tablet or laptop.

1 Train

20 minutes, 3 times a week

Short, adaptive brain games that keep memory and thinking working at the right level of challenge. Reminders keep patients on track, and a family member can help.

2 Track

10-minute check-in, every 2 weeks

The same progress test every time, so any change is real: improving, holding steady or declining. Plus short validated questionnaires on sleep, mood, stress and anxiety.

3 Act

A dashboard clinicians can use

Clinics see who's declining and needs to be seen sooner, who's improving and may have a treatable cause, and who's stable and can wait for routine review.

PATENT TO BE FILED

An adaptive AI engine at the heart of it

Most brain games are one-size-fits-all: too easy and people get bored, too hard and they give up. Interval adapts to each person as they play.

  • Tunes difficulty, speed and screen brightness in real time
  • Picks the next task from a roster based on each patient's progress
  • Keeps every patient at the edge of their ability, turning each session into data
